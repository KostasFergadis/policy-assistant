# Cash Handling

## 1.1 Scope

This policy applies to everyone who operates a till or handles company cash. Employees must complete cash handling training before they are assigned to a till.

## 2.1 Till Operation

Each employee logs on to the till with their own ID and must never share it. Tills must be locked or logged off whenever they are left unattended, even briefly. Only one employee should work on a till drawer during a shift.

## 2.2 Till Limits

No till should hold more than 500 pounds in notes. When a till goes over this limit, the employee asks the duty manager to carry out a cash lift, moving the excess to the safe in a sealed bag.

## 2.3 Counterfeit Notes

Notes of 20 pounds and above must be checked with the detector pen. If a note appears to be fake, politely decline it and ask for another form of payment. Do not accuse the customer or keep the note. Tell the duty manager once the customer has left.

## 3.1 Refunds

Refunds are given using the original payment method. Cash refunds above 50 pounds must be approved by the duty manager. A refund needs proof of purchase, and an employee must never refund their own purchase.

## 3.2 Till Differences

A difference of more than 5 pounds between the expected and counted amount must be reported to the duty manager before the end of the shift and recorded in the cash log. Three reported differences by one employee within 30 days lead to a retraining session.

## 4.1 Safe Access

Only keyholders may hold the safe code. The code is changed every 90 days and whenever a keyholder leaves the company. The safe must stay locked unless cash is actively being moved.

## 4.2 Banking

Takings are collected by the security carrier every Tuesday and Friday. If the safe holds more than 5,000 pounds between collections, the Store Manager must request an extra collection. Employees must never carry company cash to the bank themselves.

## 5.1 Robbery

If a robbery happens, stay calm and hand over the cash. Do not resist or follow the robber. Your safety comes first. Press the panic button when it is safe, call the police, and preserve the area for evidence.
